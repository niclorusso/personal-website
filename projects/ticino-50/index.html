How to read it

The five charts at the top show the whole series available. Below them, each card shows the value in the year you pick and the latest value available. If an indicator has no figure for that year, the closest year is used and shown next to the number, highlighted when it is more than two years away. The dot on the chart marks the year compared. The note on each figure opens under the chart.

For the climate, single years vary a lot, so the numbers compared are averages over the ten years ending in that year. The thin line is the yearly value, the thick line the ten-year average.

Where a grey line appears, it is the same indicator for Switzerland as a whole.

Sources

Population, naturalisations, age, marriages, wages, jobs, unemployment by the international definition, cross-border workers, rents, vacant homes, cars, farming, wood harvest, cinemas, road accidents, hotels, census, elections and votes: Federal Statistical Office (STAT-TAB and stats.swiss). The table is listed for each indicator in the data file.

Health insurance premiums: Federal Office of Public Health, statistics of compulsory health insurance, table T 3.01, until 2022; Federal Statistical Office for 2023 and 2024. Registered unemployment: State Secretariat for Economic Affairs, monthly rates by canton as published by the Federal Statistical Office. Climate: MeteoSwiss, homogenised series of the Lugano station.

Several series start after 1976 or stop early. Premiums start in 1996, when insurance became compulsory. Rents are available from 2000, wages and cross-border workers from 2002, cars and hotel stays from 2005, road accidents from 1992, vacant homes from 1997, the age of residents from 1981. Premiums and rents are compared with wages from 2010. The census figures on language, religion and households exist only for 1970, 1980, 1990 and 2000, so for those cards "today" is 2000.

Population figures come from registers since 2010 and from estimates before. Votes are compared by decade; the 2020s run to the end of 2025.
