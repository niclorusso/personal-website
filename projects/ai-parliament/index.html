    How the data is collected

    The data comes from Curia Vista, the open database of the Swiss Parliament. An item is included if its title, submitted text or justification contains one of these words: "künstliche Intelligenz", "KI", "ChatGPT", "Deepfake", words starting with "Algorithm", "Gesichtserkennung", "autonome Waffen" or "Killerroboter" in German; "intelligence artificielle", "algorithm…", "reconnaissance faciale", "armes autonomes" or "robots tueurs" in French; "intelligenza artificiale", "algoritm…", "riconoscimento facciale" or "armi autonome" in Italian. Items that mention data centres ("Rechenzentrum", "centre de données", "centro di calcolo", "data center") are included from 2023. Most of them do not mention AI: they are about energy, water, noise and permits.

    This is a keyword match. Some items only mention these words in passing. By default the page counts the items where AI is the main subject or a substantial part; the buttons at the top switch to the items mainly about AI or to every mention. Items that discuss AI without using any of these words are missing. Three checks were run on the selection. A second reading of 90 items taken at random agreed on all 40 passing mentions and on 38 of the 50 items counted as about AI; of the other 12, all classed as only partly about AI, it judged 5 not about AI and 7 borderline. Of 240 items taken at random among the 11,508 filed from 2023 to 2026 and not selected, none was about AI. A search with 357 other words found no missed item that names AI, and about 25 items on self-driving vehicles and robots, which are not included. Two items where "KI" stands for small importers were removed by hand.

    Several members sometimes file the same text on the same day. By default the page counts each text once, under the item with the lowest number, and lists it as cross-party when the members belong to several groups; untick the checkbox to count every copy.

    Whether AI is the main subject of an item, its subject, the two scores (risk or opportunity, regulate or promote) and the English summary of each item were produced by an AI model (Claude) that read the submitted text and the justification, following a written rubric. For the items that ask for rules, the kind of rule was assigned in the same way with a second rubric. Each score goes from -2 to 2; the charts show only its sign. A second pass by the same model on 60 items gave the same score nine times out of ten. This shows that the scores are stable, not that they are right. The scores are judgements, not measurements: do not use them to rank single items or members. "Asks for rules or safeguards" is broad: it includes requests to examine safeguards, and only about a third of these items ask for binding rules. People who file an item usually want something changed, so the whole set leans towards risk and regulation.

    Parliamentary group, party and canton are those in the member's current record, also for older items. The chart on regulation by group uses only items filed by members, not those filed in the name of a group or a committee. Parliament does not translate titles into English, so the English lines in the list are the model's summaries.
