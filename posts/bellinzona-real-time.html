A live map of my hometown

An illustrated map of Bellinzona built from open data, where trains, buses, weather and river follow the real town.

Open the project →

I grew up in Bellinzona, and I wanted to see it from above as it is right now. So I built a map of the town that you can drag and zoom, drawn like a model railway, where things move when they move in the real town.

How it is made

Everything comes from open data. The buildings, the terrain and the aerial photo are from swisstopo, the height of the trees from the national forest inventory, and roads, railway, bus lines and traffic lights from OpenStreetMap. From these I built a 3D model of 49 km² with 13,895 buildings and 173,057 trees, rendered it from one fixed angle, and had an image model paint over the render, one piece at a time, so that it looks hand-drawn. The centre and the area around it are painted; the edges of the map still show the plain render.

The idea is not mine: I took it from Isometric World by Dmytro Krasun, who did the same for Tel Aviv.

What is real and what is not

  • Real: the time of day, the light and the season, the weather (MeteoSwiss measurements, every ten minutes, from the station on the Magadino plain) and the level of the Ticino river (federal gauge in Bellinzona).
  • From the official timetable: trains and buses. They leave when the timetable of an ordinary weekday says, so delays and cancellations do not show.
  • Simulated: cars and people. There are more or fewer of them depending on the hour and the day, the cars stop at red lights, and on Saturday morning the market is in the old town. But they are invented, and so is the timing of the traffic lights.

The model itself is a photograph of one moment: a building site that opened last month is not there.

The labels are in Italian. The clock button lets you choose another day and hour.
