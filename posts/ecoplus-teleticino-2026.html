On TeleTicino: work, family and young people in Ticino

Oct. 30, 2026

On October 2 I was a guest on ECò Plus on TeleTicino, with Augusto Mitidieri and Nora Jardini Croci Torti, to discuss work, family and corporate welfare in Ticino. Thank you to Gianfranco De Santis for the invitation and for leading the discussion.

▶ Watch the full episode on TeleTicino (in Italian)

I am one of the many young people from Ticino who studied and now work north of the Gotthard. Before the show I looked at some numbers on this. Here they are, with what I think about them.

Many of us leave, fewer come back

In 2022/23, 934 students from Ticino were enrolled at ETH Zurich, almost as many as at USI in Lugano (943). 84% of university students from Ticino study outside the canton (DECS).

Leaving to study is not the problem. Coming back is. Five years after graduating, only 55–60% of those who studied outside Ticino live in the canton (DECS). Between 2015 and 2024, Ticino lost on average 495 people per year to other cantons, most of them to Zurich (USTAT, via tio.ch).

Salaries and cost of living

In 2024 the median gross monthly salary was 5,708 francs in Ticino and 7,502 in the Zurich region, 24% more (FSO).

People often say that life in Ticino costs less. Rent does: a 3–4 room flat costs about 410 francs less per month than in the canton of Zurich (tio.ch). But in Zurich I don't need a car. In Ticino it would be my first expense, and an average car costs about 925 francs a month (TCS).

What I think could help

Living in Ticino and working elsewhere. Bellinzona is less than two hours from Zurich by train. With two or three days of remote work a week, you can work for a company in Zurich, live in Ticino and pay your taxes there. This only works if companies judge people on results, not on hours in the office.

Real roles for those who left. Young people are usually offered spaces for young people: youth councils, under-30 prizes. I would rather see one seat for us on the board of a foundation, an association or a company. On the boards of the 100 largest Swiss companies the average age is 60 (schillingreport 2026). Most boards meet a few times a year, so you can do it from Zurich. My own tie to Ticino today is TEDxBellinzona, where I have a real responsibility.

Keep hiring young people. In my work I see AI taking over the tasks on which young people used to learn the job. In the US, employment of 22-to-25-year-olds in the jobs most exposed to AI is 19% below where it would be if it had kept pace with less exposed jobs (Stanford Digital Economy Lab). A company that stops hiring young people because "AI does it" will have no one to promote in ten years.

Work with Zurich instead of competing with it. Ticino will not win a race on salaries. It already works where it collaborates: the master's in medicine at USI was set up with ETH, the University of Zurich and the University of Basel, and ETH's supercomputing centre, CSCS, is in Lugano.
